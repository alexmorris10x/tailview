# TailView and Deploy Link App Store launch

**Status:** ⚠️ Research complete; proposed launch choices need Alex’s decision. Neither app submitted.

Research checked 30 October 2026. **Launch TailView first as a $9.99 iPhone/iPad app with a free Mac host, and publish both ends as open source. Make Deploy Link free and open source, but clarify its native installation workflow with App Review before investing in public packaging.** Neither needs a recurring customer payment. Give each a small website that explains the product and links to signed Mac downloads hosted on GitHub Releases. These are proposed choices, not approved implementation. Keep the work to publishing useful existing tools; Tapreel remains the growth focus.

| Product | Recommended iOS price | Mac distribution | Recommended source model |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TailView** | **$9.99 upfront**, iPhone and iPad | Free signed, notarized Mac app | Full iOS client and Mac host, Apache-2.0 where rights and dependencies permit |
| **Deploy Link** | **Free initially** | Free packaged Python CLI/server and LaunchAgent | Full client and CLI/server, same conditional Apache-2.0 choice |

## TailView has a credible App Store route; Deploy Link needs a specific answer

TailView fits an established category: accessing your own whole computer remotely. Jump Desktop’s iOS listing advertises worldwide access. Apple’s guideline 4.2.7 places conditions, including a local-network condition, on a particular specific-software/service mirroring case; that is distinct from generic whole-device remote desktop. This supports TailView’s route without guaranteeing approval. Keep its **Tailscale-only design** and describe what it actually does. ([Jump Desktop](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/jump-desktop-rdp-vnc-fluid/id364876095), [Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

Mandatory Tailscale deserves a clear review explanation because guideline 4.2.3(i) addresses dependence on another installed app. It is not an established automatic blocker: **Landline: Tailscale SSH** explicitly requires Tailscale on both ends and a free open-source daemon. Its current listing offers a free download, seven-day trial and $2.99 permanent unlock. That is a strong architecture precedent, although SSH is a different product and Apple does not publish its review reasoning. Embedding networking or adding public listeners is unnecessary launch scope. ([Landline listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/landline-tailscale-ssh/id6808344753))

Deploy Link carries a more consequential interpretation risk. Its main action hands an HTTPS installation manifest to iOS through `itms-services` to install another signed application. Guideline **2.5.2** and Developer Program License Agreement **3.3.1(B)** restrict downloading/installing executable code. Saying that code does not execute inside Deploy Link does not dispose of the issue. Conversely, TestApp.io has an App Store client whose official instructions describe installing development and Ad Hoc builds. That precedent makes a categorical rejection prediction unjustified. ([Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [license agreement](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/), [TestApp.io client](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/testapp-io/id1518972541), [installation guide](https://help.testapp.io/mobile-app-getting-started/), [accepted certificate types](https://help.testapp.io/faq/ios-certificate-types/))

Before expensive packaging, take this exact question and a physical-device demonstration video to an **App Review appointment**: “Can an App Store client list a developer’s already-signed development or Ad Hoc builds on their own private Tailscale server and invoke `itms-services` for devices already covered by their provisioning profiles, without signing, modifying or bypassing Apple’s installation checks? How do 2.5.2 and Agreement 3.3.1(B) apply?” An appointment provides guidance, **not preapproval**. If that route is unsuitable, a private Safari installation portal preserves the useful queue; it still follows Apple’s distribution rules. Do not hide installation functionality during review or assume a Safari-forwarding button settles the policy question. ([App Review appointments](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/app-review-appointment), [Diawi’s Safari workflow](https://www.diawi.com/knowledge-base/Diawi/Getting-started))

Deploy Link must promise convenience for **properly signed developer/test builds**, not arbitrary IPA installation. Device registration, provisioning coverage, certificate/profile expiry and Developer Mode where applicable still apply. The signing developer needs the appropriate Apple membership; every invited tester does not need a separate paid developer account. Apple currently limits registered devices to 100 per product family per membership year. App Store availability of the client changes none of these conditions. ([Registered-device distribution](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/distributing-your-app-to-registered-devices), [device limits](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/devices/devices-overview/), [Developer Mode](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/enabling-developer-mode-on-a-device))

## Charge once for TailView’s convenience; keep Deploy Link friction low

**$9.99 is a price hypothesis, not validated demand.** Jump Desktop costs $14.99 on iPhone/iPad; Screens 5’s lifetime option is $199.99 across iPhone, iPad, Mac and Vision Pro. Those products differ substantially in scope and maturity. TailView should sell a focused, inspectable way to reach your own Mac, not imply feature parity. Tailscale alone is not unique: Screens already supports it. The [price comparison](2026-10-30%20-%20Research%20-%20Companion%20app%20launch%20assets/pricing-comparison.html) shows the different platform scopes on one honest linear scale. ([Jump price](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/jump-desktop-rdp-vnc-fluid/id364876095), [Screens pricing](https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/faq/s5-pricing), [Screens connectivity](https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/getting-started/connecting))

Paid upfront is the simplest release because neither repository contains a located StoreKit implementation. A free connection trial plus one permanent non-consumable unlock would let customers establish compatibility before paying, but adds purchase, restore and entitlement work. Reserve that change for evidence of onboarding friction. Say **“One purchase; no TailView subscription”**, not “lifetime unlimited support.” Apple supports both paid apps and non-expiring non-consumables. Publisher membership remains a separate recurring operating cost, currently US$99 annually; the customer need not subscribe. ([Apple business models](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/business-models/), [membership](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/membership/program-enrollment/))

Deploy Link’s narrow self-hosted utility competes with free distribution options, including Firebase App Distribution and TestApp.io’s free tier. Charging receiving users adds friction before willingness to pay is established. **Free is the better initial choice**; $4.99 upfront is a fallback only if collecting revenue takes priority. No premium tiers, hosted service or additional feature roadmap follows from this recommendation. ([Firebase pricing](https://firebase.google.com/pricing), [TestApp.io pricing](https://testapp.io/pricing/))

Tailscale has separate plans and terms: its free Personal plan is for non-commercial use. Some connections use encrypted DERP relays, so avoid “always direct,” “no cloud,” or “all networking is free.” Suitable wording is: “View and control your Mac over your own Tailscale network. The Mac companion is free; Tailscale is required and has its own plans.” ([Tailscale pricing](https://tailscale.com/pricing), [encrypted relays](https://tailscale.com/docs/reference/derp-servers))

## Open both products, without turning source visibility into a security claim

**Publish TailView’s client and host**, because both handle sensitive activity; host-only publication leaves important behavior unavailable for inspection. Publish Deploy Link too: its developer audience benefits from inspecting how build artifacts and installation requests are handled. For Alex-owned code with compatible dependencies, **Apache-2.0** permits paid binaries and commercial reuse, includes an explicit contributor patent grant, and avoids designing App Store exceptions around copyleft. It also allows free self-builds and commercial forks. Sell the official signed build, convenient updates and ordinary support, not exclusivity. ([Apache-2.0](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0))

GPL is not categorically incompatible with commercial App Store distribution—KeePassium demonstrates a carefully managed arrangement—but rights, dependencies and contributor permissions make it a more involved default. A restriction against commercial forks would be source-available, not open source. Whatever license is chosen, public code establishes an opportunity for inspection, **not an audit or proof that a binary matches its source**. Publish tagged source, build instructions, notices and a clear description of access boundaries. Sanitize any history before publication or release a reviewed source snapshot. ([KeePassium licensing](https://github.com/keepassium/KeePassium), [open-source definition](https://opensource.org/osd), [source-to-binary verification](https://reproducible-builds.org/))

BlackHole currently remains an **independent optional installation**, not bundled TailView code or an installer. It supports sending the iOS microphone into a Mac input; screen viewing and receiving Mac sound work without it. Preserve that arrangement. Any future bundling needs a separate rights check; this research found no basis to assert an existing licensing violation. ([Current integration](https://github.com/alexmorris10x/tailview/blob/e97cb8c918c2b175980f0f6b6ecd06a0bfb3fe00/MacHost/AudioSink.swift#L1-L27), [BlackHole terms](https://github.com/ExistentialAudio/BlackHole/blob/master/LICENSE))

## Use websites for understanding and GitHub Releases for downloads

A website answers “what is this, what does it cost, which permissions does it need, and how do I install or remove it?” **GitHub Releases can host the actual downloads**, version history and matching source tags. These serve complementary purposes. Both repositories are currently private, with no declared license or GitHub release assets; private releases do not become public because a website links them. ([GitHub release access](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/releasing-projects-on-github/about-releases))

TailView’s Mac host should ship as a Developer ID-signed, hardened-runtime, notarized and stapled app/DMG. Its Accessibility control and private Mac display APIs make the **Mac App Store a poor fit today**: that store requires sandboxing and public APIs. These Mac-only implementation choices do not automatically prohibit the separately built iOS client. Direct distribution still needs an explicit compatibility decision for the private display features. ([Apple sandbox restrictions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/protecting-user-data-with-app-sandbox), [notarization](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/notarizing-macos-software-before-distribution), [Mac/iOS target separation](https://github.com/alexmorris10x/tailview/blob/e97cb8c918c2b175980f0f6b6ecd06a0bfb3fe00/project.yml#L9-L25))

Deploy Link has **no current Mac GUI**. Package its actual Python CLI/server and LaunchAgent with explicit runtime, installation, update and uninstall behavior; a signed/notarized installer is a suitable route. Start with documented versioned downloads. TailView can add signed Sparkle updates when warranted. Keep the initial supported host Mac-only; Linux is unpromised future scope. ([Deploy Link installer](https://github.com/alexmorris10x/deploy-link/blob/c7d12a10373db942742de8cdab6bc636f3177298/scripts/install_ota_queue.sh#L11-L62), [Sparkle guidance](https://sparkle-project.org/documentation/))

## Launch after concrete gates, with TailView first

**First, settle the choices and clarify Deploy Link’s review route in parallel.** Neither app had an App Store Connect record visible to the configured account/key for its Release bundle ID. This is account-scoped evidence, not proof about every Apple team. The research changed no repository visibility, license, account or submission.

**Then close the specific source gaps.** TailView already has Keychain pairing, revoke and persistent pause. Make control/microphone behavior an informed opt-in, including automatic reconnect and system audio routing. Rotate or remove the legacy UUID view-code path that currently survives a closed pairing window. Resolve the silent-playback review risk: background execution must correspond to intentional audio use, with clear stop/mute behavior. Resolve Tailscale CLI discovery and Screen Recording/Accessibility onboarding. ([Pairing and legacy admission](https://github.com/alexmorris10x/tailview/blob/e97cb8c918c2b175980f0f6b6ecd06a0bfb3fe00/MacHost/VideoServer.swift#L413-L455), [silent playback](https://github.com/alexmorris10x/tailview/blob/e97cb8c918c2b175980f0f6b6ecd06a0bfb3fe00/IOSViewer/ViewerModel.swift#L1220-L1237))

For Deploy Link, replace the personal hostname default and choose explicit paired/scoped access, or a clearly limited personal-use contract enforced by narrow tailnet access rules. Today reachability authorizes queue reads **and deletion**; a UI confirmation is not server authorization. Keep the listener private behind Tailscale Serve. Correct “Installed” to “downloaded” or “installation requested”: completed transfer does not establish installation. One host is enough; do not add multi-host scope. ([Current API boundary](https://github.com/alexmorris10x/deploy-link/blob/c7d12a10373db942742de8cdab6bc636f3177298/ota/ota_queue.py#L515-L589), [transfer-only receipt](https://github.com/alexmorris10x/deploy-link/blob/c7d12a10373db942742de8cdab6bc636f3177298/ota/ota_queue.py#L592-L628))

**Next, produce usable distribution and review materials.** Demonstrate setup on an independent Mac/device, permissions, pairing, revoke/pause and removal. Publish accurate privacy/support pages and source notices; check applicable privacy manifests and IPv6-only behavior. Give reviewers an isolated demonstration Mac, workable Tailscale instructions and a physical-device video, without exposing Alex’s personal environment. Ask whether a full-featured demo mode is acceptable if access cannot be arranged. ([Apple review information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review/))

**Finally, submit only through Alex’s release step.** This research ran no builds, installation tests or independent security assessment. Obtain a focused independent assessment before making security assurance claims. The actionable destination is a usable, honestly described TailView release, with Deploy Link proceeding through the review route Apple clarifies or its private web fallback. The exact prices remain hypotheses to revise against actual purchases and support friction.
