How TailView handles your data.

TailView connects devices you choose. It does not run a remote desktop service or receive your screen, audio, or typing on a developer-operated server.

Effective October 30, 2026. Covers the TailView iOS viewer, TailView Mac host, and this website. Developer: Alex Morris.

What crosses the connection

While sharing is active, the Mac sends images of its main display to paired devices. If enabled, it also sends Mac app and media audio. A paired device sends remote input such as pointer movement, key presses, and pasted text. If you enable microphone return and grant permission, that device sends microphone audio to the Mac.

Ordinary remote sessions are streamed, not recorded into an archive by TailView. Other apps on your Mac can receive or record the input you choose to give them. Pasting replaces the Mac’s plain-text clipboard; do not paste a password or secret into an unintended window.

The app protocol relies on the Tailscale network for encrypted transport; it does not add a separate TLS layer. Tailscale may connect devices directly or carry encrypted traffic through a relay. TailView is not affiliated with Tailscale. Its privacy policy and terms cover its service, including network coordination.

What stays on your devices

  • Pairing: the iOS Keychain stores saved Mac names, addresses, device identifiers, and pairing credentials. The Mac Keychain stores paired-device names, identifiers, pairing dates, and credential digests.
  • Preferences: audio, interface, access-pause, and display settings are stored locally. The host stores recovery state to restore audio routes and display settings after an interrupted session.
  • Local diagnostics: connection and audio events can appear in system logs. The iOS app also keeps a rotating diagnostic file of about 1 MB in its cache. It can contain timestamps, app state, audio-device names, and error messages. It is not automatically uploaded to the developer.
  • Optional microphone tests: a diagnostic recorder test temporarily saves about four seconds of audio on the iOS device, measures it, and attempts to remove the sample after the test. An interrupted test may leave it in temporary storage until cleanup. A diagnostic result can be stored as local text. These files are not uploaded automatically.
  • Purchases: the app uses Apple’s verified StoreKit transaction information to determine trial dates and lifetime access. Apple handles checkout and payment information. TailView does not receive your payment-card details.

No advertising or analytics SDK

TailView does not include advertising, tracking, or a developer analytics service. It does not require a TailView account. Apple may provide crash or usage diagnostics according to your Apple settings and its policies; those are separate from TailView’s local logs.

Using our support links is your choice. GitHub processes the information you post under its privacy statement. This website is hosted by GitHub Pages; GitHub may process ordinary request information such as IP addresses. The site includes no analytics scripts, tracking pixels, or third-party fonts.

You decide when sharing stops

Disconnect on iOS ends that session. Pause Access on the Mac disconnects all viewers, stops screen capture and remote input, and persists after relaunch. Revoke on the Mac removes a paired device’s access. Forget on iOS removes the saved Mac profile from that device. Use both when retiring a pairing.

Revoking Microphone permission in iOS Settings stops the app’s access to that microphone. macOS Privacy & Security controls screen capture and Accessibility permissions. Keychain records can survive app deletion, so remove pairings in the app before uninstalling. The setup guide explains the complete uninstall sequence.

A darkened Mac display is still an active, unlocked desktop unless macOS is separately locked. TailView’s display controls are not a substitute for device access controls or disk encryption.

Questions and changes

Contact the developer through TailView support on GitHub. Issues are public: ask a general question first and never include credentials, pairing codes, screenshots of personal content, or raw logs. If private detail is necessary, ask for a private contact route before sharing it.
